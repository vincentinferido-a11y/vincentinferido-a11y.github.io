A customer hears about you from a friend, searches your name, and lands on… a Facebook page from 2021. Or nothing at all. So they call the business that did show up.

That's the quiet cost of not having a decent website. The good news: picking the best website builder for small business use doesn't take a developer or a big budget anymore. It takes about ten minutes and the right questions.

I build custom web apps for a living, and I'll be honest: most small businesses don't need one for their website. A good website builder will get you online faster and cheaper. This guide compares the five most popular ones with real prices from their official pages, the fees nobody mentions up front, and a simple way to choose.

The 5 website builders, reviewed

Wix

The easiest way for a non-designer to get a good-looking site online.

Easiest to use
Best for
Service businesses, local shops and first-time site owners who want to drag, drop and publish.
Pricing
Light $17, Core $29, Business $39, Business Elite $159 per month, billed yearly. Monthly billing costs more.
Free plan / trial
Free plan forever (shows Wix branding and uses a username.wixsite.com address). Paid plans include a free domain for one year and a 14-day money-back guarantee.
Watch out for
Card payments through Wix Payments in the US are 2.9% + $0.30 (3.7% + $0.30 for American Express). Your domain renews at its normal price after the first year.

  • check_circleBooking, store and marketing tools in one place

  • cancelPrices climb quickly for stores with advanced needs
  • cancelMoving your site to another platform later is hard

Verdict: The safest default for most small businesses that want a nice site without learning anything technical.

Beautiful templates that make small brands look established.

Best design
Best for
Salons, studios, restaurants, photographers and any brand where looks sell.

The platform built for selling first, website second.

Best for online stores
Best for
Businesses whose website is mainly a shop: physical products, inventory, shipping and lots of orders.

No free plan. Free for 3 days, then $1/month for the first 3 months on selected plans.

Businesses that want a truly custom design, often with a designer's help, and to edit content themselves afterwards.

Monthly prices look small. Multiply them by twelve, then add the extras. Here's the first-year subscription cost for each builder's entry plan, billed yearly:

Option Year-one cost What you get
WordPress.com Personal $48 Custom domain (free for year one), no ads, plugins
Webflow Basic $180 Custom domain, up to 300 static pages, no CMS
Wix Light $204 Custom domain (free for year one), 2 GB storage
Shopify Basic $228 Full online store
Squarespace Basic Varies by country About $16/month or more per Squarespace's own guide

Before tax, card processing and paid apps. Domains renew at their normal price from year two.

The real cost is your time

A $4/month site you never finish costs more than a $29/month site you publish this weekend. Pick the builder you'll actually enjoy using, and launch a simple version first: home, services, about, contact. You can add pages later.

How to choose: 6 questions that decide it

  1. Are you selling products online? If yes, start with Shopify (or Wix, or WordPress.com's Commerce plan for smaller catalogs).
  2. How much time will you spend on it? Little time: Wix or Squarespace. Some time to learn: WordPress.com. A designer involved: Webflow.
  3. What will it cost per year at your size? Include the domain renewal, paid apps and transaction fees, not just the monthly sticker price.
  4. Do you need bookings, memberships or a blog? Check which plan includes them. Many builders gate these behind higher tiers.
  5. Can you take your content with you? WordPress is the easiest to move; most other builders are harder to leave.
  6. Who will update it? If your team will edit it weekly, ease of editing matters more than design freedom.
warning

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing on the free plan alone. Free plans show branding or ads and use the builder's address. Budget for a paid plan from day one.
  • Forgetting transaction fees. A 2–8% platform fee on top of card processing adds up fast on real sales.
  • Ignoring the renewal price. First-year domains and promos don't last forever.
  • Overbuilding. Five clear pages beat twenty half-finished ones.
  • Picking a store platform when you only need a brochure site, or the other way round.

When a website builder isn't enough

A website builder is the right start for nearly every small business. It stops fitting when your "website" is really an app:

  • Customers need accounts, dashboards or portals with their own data
  • You need custom booking or quoting logic the builder can't handle
  • Your site must connect deeply to your CRM, inventory or internal tools
  • You're paying for several apps and plugins to fake one custom feature

FAQ

What is the best website builder for a small business?expand_more

For most small businesses, Wix is the easiest all-rounder. Choose Squarespace if design matters most, Shopify if you mainly sell products online, WordPress.com for the lowest yearly price and the most flexibility, and Webflow if you want a custom design with a designer's help.

How much does a small business website cost with a website builder?expand_more

Entry plans in this guide range from $4/month (WordPress.com Personal) to $19/month (Shopify Basic) billed yearly, roughly $48 to $228 for the first year before tax. Add domain renewals from year two, paid apps, and card processing if you sell online.

Can I use a free website builder for my business?expand_more

You can start on a free plan to build and test, but free plans show the builder's branding or ads and use their web address (like yourname.wixsite.com). For a business, a paid plan with your own domain looks far more trustworthy.

Which website builder is best for selling online?expand_more

Shopify is built for selling first and is the strongest choice for product stores. Wix and WordPress.com's Commerce plan also work for smaller catalogs. Check transaction fees on lower plans before you commit.

Is WordPress.com the same as WordPress.org?expand_more

No. WordPress.com is a hosted service with plans and support, which is what this guide covers. WordPress.org is the free software you install on your own hosting, where you handle updates, security and backups yourself.

Do I need a developer to build my website?expand_more

Usually not. All five builders let you build without code, and Wix and Squarespace are the easiest. You may want a developer or designer for a custom Webflow design, or when your site needs app-like features such as customer accounts or custom booking logic.

The bottom line

  • Easiest all-rounder: Wix.
  • Best-looking with the least effort: Squarespace.
  • Selling products online: Shopify.
  • Lowest yearly price, most flexibility: WordPress.com.
  • Custom design control: Webflow.
