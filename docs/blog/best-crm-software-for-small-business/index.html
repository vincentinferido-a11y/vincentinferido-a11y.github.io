Somewhere in your inbox is a lead who said "sounds great, let me think about it"… and never heard from you again.

It's not that you didn't care. It's that the follow-up lived in your head, a sticky note, or row 214 of a spreadsheet. That's the real job of a CRM: it remembers every customer and every promised follow-up so you don't have to.

I build custom CRMs for a living, so you might expect me to tell you to build one. I won't, at least not yet. For most small businesses, an off-the-shelf CRM is the right first step. This guide will help you pick the right one in about ten minutes, with real prices and none of the marketing fog.

What a CRM really costs: a 5-person team example

Sticker prices are per seat, per month, so multiply before you fall in love. Here's the monthly bill for a 5-person team on annual billing, using the prices above:

Option Monthly cost for 5 users Notes
Freshsales Growth $45 5 × $9
Bitrix24 Basic $49 Flat, includes 5 users
Zoho CRM Standard $70 5 × $14
Salesforce Starter Suite $125 5 × $25
Pipedrive Growth $195 5 × $39
HubSpot Sales Hub Professional $450 5 × $90, plus $1,500 one-time onboarding (about $6,900 in year one)

Before tax. HubSpot's Starter promo ($7/seat) would be $35/month, but it's a new-customer price, so check what it renews at.

lightbulb

The cheapest CRM is the one your team actually uses

A $70/month CRM that nobody updates is more expensive than a $195/month CRM that runs your whole sales process. Use the free trial with real deals, not demo data, and ask your team which one they'd open every day.

What actually matters when choosing

Forget the 200-feature comparison charts. For a small business, these six questions decide it:

  1. Will my team use it daily? Adoption beats features. Try it with real work during the trial.
  2. What does it cost at my size in a year? Multiply seats, include add-ons and onboarding fees, and check the renewal price.
  3. Does it connect to what I already use? Email (Gmail or Outlook), calendar, website forms, accounting and your phone system.
  4. Can I get my data out? Make sure you can export contacts and deals if you ever switch.
  5. Does it work on a phone? If your team is on the road, the mobile app matters more than the dashboard.
  6. Does it handle privacy properly? See the next section, especially if you sell to Europe or California.

Privacy: the part most guides skip

A CRM is a database of people, and that comes with legal responsibilities:

  • Europe (GDPR): GDPR applies to EU businesses and to businesses outside the EU that offer goods or services to people in the EU or monitor their behavior. If you have EU customers, look for data-residency options, a data processing agreement (DPA), and tools to export or delete a person's data on request.
  • California (CCPA): It applies to for-profit businesses that meet certain thresholds, such as over $25 million in annual revenue. It gives consumers the right to know what data you hold, delete it, correct it and opt out of its sale or sharing.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check with a qualified professional for your situation.

warning

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying for the business you hope to be in five years. Choose for the next 12–18 months. You can upgrade later.
  • Importing a messy spreadsheet as-is. Clean duplicates and old leads first, or you'll pay per contact for junk.
  • Skipping the setup day. Define your pipeline stages and required fields before inviting the team.
  • Ignoring renewal pricing. Promo and first-year prices don't last forever.
  • Paying for seats nobody uses. Review active users every quarter.

When an off-the-shelf CRM isn't enough

Most small businesses should start with one of the tools above. A custom CRM starts to make sense when:

  • You're paying for features you don't use while hacking around features you do need
  • Your process is truly unique (special approvals, industry workflows, bookings plus sales plus operations)
  • Per-seat costs are climbing fast as the team grows
  • You need your CRM tightly connected to your own app, portal or database

FAQ

What is the best free CRM for a small business?expand_more

HubSpot's free CRM (up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts, no credit card) is the easiest to start with. Zoho CRM's free edition supports up to 3 users, and Bitrix24 offers a free plan for 1–2 users. Pick based on team size and which one feels easiest to use.

How much should a small business spend on a CRM?expand_more

For a 5-person team, paid plans in this guide range from about $45 to $195 per month on annual billing, and more for advanced tiers like HubSpot Sales Hub Professional. Start with the cheapest plan that covers your must-haves, then upgrade when you hit a real limit.

Is it better to pay monthly or annually?expand_more

Annual billing is cheaper (Zoho advertises up to 34% savings, Pipedrive up to 26%, Freshsales about 20%). Pay monthly while you're testing, and switch to annual once your team is committed.

Do I need a CRM if I only have a few customers?expand_more

If you can remember every follow-up without effort, not yet. Once you start losing track of leads, forgetting follow-ups, or sharing customer info across people, a free CRM plan pays for itself.

Can I switch CRMs later?expand_more

Yes. Most CRMs let you export contacts and deals, usually as CSV files. Check export options during your trial, keep your data clean, and switching will be much easier.

When should I build a custom CRM instead?expand_more

When your process doesn't fit standard tools, you're paying for many unused seats or features, or your CRM needs to connect deeply to your own systems. Start with an off-the-shelf tool, learn what you really need, then decide.

The bottom line

  • Just starting? HubSpot Free or Zoho's free edition.
  • Tight budget, small team? Zoho CRM or Freshsales.
  • Sales pipeline is everything? Pipedrive.
  • Planning to scale big? Salesforce Starter Suite.
  • Bigger team, one flat bill? Bitrix24.

Pick one, start a trial this week with real leads, and give it two weeks. The best CRM is the one that means you never lose another "let me think about it" lead.
