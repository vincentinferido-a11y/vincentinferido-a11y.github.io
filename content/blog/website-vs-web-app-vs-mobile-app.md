"We need an app" is one of the most common requests I hear, and one of the most expensive to get wrong. Sometimes a business truly needs a mobile app. Often a website or a web app would solve the same problem faster, for a fraction of the cost, and reach more people.

Here's how to tell the difference.

## The three options in plain English

### Website
A website **presents information**. It tells people who you are, what you offer, and how to contact you or buy. Visitors mostly *read* and *click*.

*Examples:* a company site, a restaurant menu, a portfolio, a landing page for a product.

### Web app
A web app **lets people do things**. They log in, enter data, and the app remembers it. It runs in the browser, so it works on any phone, tablet or computer with no download.

*Examples:* a CRM, a booking system, an online store with accounts, a dashboard for your team.

### Mobile app
A mobile app is **installed from the App Store or Google Play**. It can use phone features more deeply, such as push notifications, the camera, offline mode and background location.

*Examples:* a delivery driver app, a fitness tracker, a banking app.

## Side-by-side

| | Website | Web app | Mobile app |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Main job** | Inform and convert | Let users do tasks | Frequent, on-the-go use |
| **Needs a download?** | No | No | Yes |
| **Works on** | Everything with a browser | Everything with a browser | iPhone and/or Android |
| **Found on Google?** | Yes, great for SEO | Public pages can be | Not directly |
| **Relative cost** | Lowest | Medium | Highest (often two platforms) |
| **Time to launch** | Days to weeks | Weeks | Weeks to months, plus app-store review |
| **Updates** | Instant | Instant | Users must update the app |

## A simple decision guide

Answer these in order:

1. **Do visitors just need information and a way to contact you or buy?** → A **website** is enough.
2. **Do users need to log in, save data or manage something** (bookings, orders, clients, projects)? → A **web app**.
3. **Will the same people use it many times a day, on the move, and need things only a phone can do** (push notifications, camera, offline, GPS in the background)? → Consider a **mobile app**.

If you're unsure between a web app and a mobile app, the answer is usually **start with a web app**.

## The most expensive mistake: building a mobile app first

Mobile apps feel "serious", but they come with real extra costs:

- You often need **two versions** (iPhone and Android), or a cross-platform build that still needs testing on both.
- **App-store review** adds delays to every release.
- Users must **find, download and keep** the app. Every step loses people.
- Every bug fix requires users to **update**.

Meanwhile, a web app reaches everyone instantly through a link you can put in emails, social posts and QR codes.

**A smarter path:** launch a web app, learn what your customers actually use, and build a mobile app later, only if the data shows people need it.

## The middle ground: a Progressive Web App (PWA)

A PWA is a web app that can be **added to the phone's home screen**, opens full-screen like an app, can work partly offline, and can send notifications on many devices. For many businesses, it gives most of the "app feel" without the app-store cost and friction.

It's not right for everything (heavy games or apps needing deep hardware access are better native), but it's worth considering before committing to a full mobile app.

## Real-world examples

| Business need | Best fit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A café wants people to see the menu and location | Website | Information and directions only |
| A clinic wants patients to book and pay deposits | Web app | Accounts, bookings and payments; works from any phone |
| A sales team needs to track leads and follow-ups | Web app (CRM) | Used at a desk and on phones; no download needed |
| A delivery company needs drivers to scan parcels offline | Mobile app | Camera, offline mode and background GPS |
| A startup testing a new idea | Web app or PWA | Fastest and cheapest way to learn what works |

## The bottom line

- **Website:** you want to be found and contacted.
- **Web app:** your customers or team need to *do* things and save data.
- **Mobile app:** frequent, on-the-go use that genuinely needs phone features.

Start with the simplest option that solves the problem. You can always grow from there, and you'll grow based on real usage instead of guesses.
